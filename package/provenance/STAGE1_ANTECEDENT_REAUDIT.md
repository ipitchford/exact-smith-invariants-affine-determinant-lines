## 1. Initial Mode-7 block

The academic-pipeline failure-mode checklist defines Mode 7 as frame-lock at an
early pipeline stage: a premature research framing is inherited by later
stages even after contrary evidence becomes available. That risk materialised
here at the literature boundary.

The initial Stage-1 corpus was strongest on polynomial Chinese-remainder
determinants, generalized Sylvester/Koszul constructions,
determinant-of-complexes formalisms, projective factorisation and incidence
matrices. It did not adequately search the direct coefficient-product
Jacobian, Hensel-lifting, polynomial-multiplication singularity and
coincident-root-locus routes. The writing handoff nevertheless described the
substantive source search as completed. Continuing without correction would
have locked the paper into an over-broad account of what was new.

The Mode-7 response was therefore blocking rather than cosmetic:

1. freeze any stronger priority interpretation of the existing wording;
2. reopen Stage 1 at the level of exact mathematical objects;
3. search direct sources and alias families;
4. compare domain, codomain, normalisation, coefficient directions,
   determinant size, factor count and scheme-theoretic strength; and
5. remediate the canonical source and assurance records before treating the
   bounded claim boundary as current.

The block is resolved only in this limited sense: the direct antecedents are
now recorded and no fatal exact collision was found. Specialist priority
resolution remains outstanding.

## 2. Questions audited

The re-audit separated four objects that keyword-level searches can easily
conflate:

1. the full non-monic affine all-factor maximal-minor/Plücker tensor;
2. the multi-border determinant obtained by contracting that tensor;
3. the pairwise-resultant character lattice and its complete-edge Smith normal
   form; and
4. the generic degree of a semi-invariant-normalised factorisation map.

For each candidate source, the audit asked whether it treated:

- two factors or arbitrary labelled factors;
- monic, one-factor-normalised, projective, or fully affine coefficient space;
- a square Jacobian determinant, a rank criterion, one specialised maximal
  minor, or the simultaneous complementary-minor tensor;
- a scalar resultant, a torus-kernel Plücker coordinate, a bordered
  contraction, or an integer character lattice; and
- geometric point counts or scheme degree, including inseparability.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Discovery and alias search

The focused search used combinations of:

- polynomial multiplication, coefficient product, Cauchy product and
  convolution map;
- Jacobian, differential, maximal minors, complementary minors, Fitting ideal,
  determinant line and Plücker coordinates;
- Sylvester matrix, resultant matrix, Hensel lemma, coprime factorisation and
  product equations;
- coincident-root loci, multiple-root loci, polynomial-multiplication
  singularities and projective tangent maps;
- bordered Jacobian, augmented determinant, multi-border and vertical
  Jacobian;
- resultant semi-invariant, torus character, character lattice and Smith
  normal form; and
- weighted signless incidence, arithmetical graph, tree and odd-unicyclic
  minors.

Backward references and bibliographic aliases around the newly located direct
sources were checked to the extent available in the bounded web corpus.

### 3.2 Verification standard

Metadata was reconciled against publisher records, author-hosted or
open-access full text, NUMDAM, arXiv, and stable Stacks Project tags. A source
was assigned to a claim only after the relevant lemma, theorem, example or page
was inspected. Similar vocabulary without matching domain, normalisation and
determinant object was classified as adjacent rather than a collision.

This was a focused producer audit, not a systematic review with a complete
database export, independent dual screening, citation-network closure, or a
specialist's priority opinion.

## 5. Claim-by-claim adjudication

### 5.1 Non-monic affine all-factor Plücker tensor

**Result: exact collision not found.**

Bhargava–Cremona–Fisher–Gajović Lemma 2.6(b) is the closest direct partial
antecedent. Fixing one factor to be monic removes one source coefficient and
turns the two-factor affine map square; its determinant is one specialised
coordinate of the full complementary-minor vector. Artin, Basu–Pollack–Roy and
Stacks give square slices, while Chaperon–López de Medrano and Chipalkatti give
rank-locus information. None of the inspected sources states, for arbitrary
labelled factors, every signed maximal minor simultaneously as the product of
all pairwise resultants times the matching torus-kernel Plücker coordinate,
with integral orientation and arbitrary base change.

**Classification:** direct partial antecedents plus adjacent frameworks; no
fatal collision.

### 5.2 Multi-border determinant

**Result: exact antecedent not found.**

Once the Plücker tensor is known, the multi-border formula follows formally by
Laplace expansion and Cauchy–Binet. The bounded search did not locate this
contraction in the present factorisation architecture.

**Classification:** not found exactly, but a formal corollary rather than an
independent headline priority claim.

### 5.3 Pairwise-resultant character lattice and Smith form

**Result: exact antecedent not found.**

The bihomogeneity of a pairwise resultant, and therefore its torus character,
is classical. Ordinary signless-incidence minors and Smith theory are also
classical. No inspected source gives the degree-weighted complete-edge
resultant-character lattice, its invariant-factor shape, residual
diagonalizable group, graph-minor index, or closed bad-prime support criterion.

**Classification:** classical inputs and a factorisation-specific weighted
bridge; the complete-edge Smith theorem and bad-prime criterion remain the
strongest distinct candidate contribution in this paper.

### 5.4 Generic normalised factorisation degree

**Result: no exact combined statement found; component results are standard.**

The factor \(D!/\prod_i d_i!\) is the classical labelled root-partition degree.
The factor \(\lvert\det W\rvert\) is the scheme order of the kernel of the torus
homomorphism described by \(W\), read from its Smith form. Their product is a
useful application of the present architecture but should not be marketed as
a standalone novelty.

**Classification:** synthesis of classical geometry and standard torus
arithmetic.

## 7. Revised contribution boundary

The strongest defensible bounded statement is:

> The square and normalised two-factor multiplication-Jacobian identities,
> the monic arbitrary-factor rank criterion, the multifactor scalar
> product-of-resultants determinant, projective root partitions and ordinary
> incidence theory are antecedents. The candidate contribution is restricted
> to the simultaneous universal non-monic affine all-factor
> complementary-minor/Plücker refinement and, more distinctly, the
> degree-weighted complete-edge resultant-character Smith calculation with its
> bad-prime criterion. The multi-border and generic-degree statements are
> structural corollaries or syntheses.

This is a bounded negative-search boundary, not a claim of absolute novelty.

## 8. Fatal-collision conclusion

**No fatal exact collision was located.**

The new sources materially weaken any novelty framing attached merely to
“Jacobian of multiplication equals resultant,” square/monic charts, or
pairwise-coprime regularity. They do not state the complete non-monic
all-factor tensor or weighted complete-edge Smith result inspected here.

## 9. Limitations and assurance

- The audit was performed inside the same producer/research process as the
  paper and parent candidate.
- Web and full-text inspection verified source identity and the cited local
  statements; it did not independently verify the present proofs.
- The search was bounded and did not close every citation graph or search
  subscription-only historical databases exhaustively.
- Equivalent statements may exist under determinant-of-complexes,
  Hensel-lifting, singularity-theory, toric, logarithmic, free-divisor,
  prehomogeneous-vector-space, quiver or arithmetic-graph language.
- “Not found” is not evidence of nonexistence or priority.
- No external specialist, referee, proof assistant or independent reproducer
  participated in this re-audit.
- The parent candidate remains immutable and unrefereed. This is a separately
  linked follow-up provenance record, not a correction silently folded into
  the parent object.
