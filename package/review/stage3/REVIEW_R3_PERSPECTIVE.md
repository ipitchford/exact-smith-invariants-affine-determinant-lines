## Perspective Review Report (Peer Reviewer 3)

### Reviewer Identity

I am reviewing as an arithmetic combinatorialist specialising in Smith normal forms of graph matrices, lattice saturation, arithmetic and signed-graphic matroids, toric character lattices, and finite diagonalizable group schemes. My perspective is deliberately adjacent to, rather than expert in, the manuscript's resultant geometry. I therefore concentrate on Theorems 6.1 and 7.1, Corollary 7.2, and the translation from the complete edge-character list to normalised factorisation charts.

### Overall Recommendation

**Major Revision**

### Confidence Score

**4/5.** I am highly confident in the lattice, Smith-form, signed-incidence, arithmetic-matroid, and group-scheme comments below. My confidence is lower on the priority and proof details of the affine Plücker/resultant part, which lie outside this reviewer's assigned perspective. The external literature check was targeted rather than systematic, so it should not be read as a priority determination.

### Summary Assessment

The manuscript contains a sound and potentially publishable arithmetic-combinatorial core, but that core is not yet stated in its strongest or most standard language. Theorem 6.1 is a clean degree-twisted reduction to classical signless-incidence minors; its support pattern belongs naturally to signed-graphic/frame-matroid theory, while the degree-balance and resultant interpretation are specific to this factorisation problem. Theorem 7.1 has real content: for primitive degrees, the complete edge-character cokernel is cyclic, and scaling by the common gcd gives the displayed almost-cyclic Smith shape. However, defining \(h(\mathbf e)\) as the gcd of maximal minors makes the assertion that the last Smith entry is \(h\) formally automatic. In arithmetic-matroid language, \(h\) is the standard full-list multiplicity or saturation index. Corollary 7.2 is the genuinely explicit arithmetic result, because it computes the prime support rather than merely naming a determinantal divisor.

I recommend major revision because a short \(p\)-local star presentation appears to determine every missing valuation and to simplify Theorem 7.1 substantially. The paper must also distinguish the lattice generated by all resultants from a square subset of individual resultant normalisers: \(h=1\) need not imply that any \(k-1\) individual edges form a unimodular basis. The availability of this local formula makes the revision more promising, but does not reduce my recommendation to minor revision: it changes the main theorem, proof strategy, examples, and contribution framing. With these changes and the arithmetic-matroid/toric-arrangement context added, the Smith-normal-form direction remains the paper's highest-value credible conclusion, more so than the derived affine lift or the generic-degree synthesis.

### Strengths (3-5 items)

1. **Transparent weighted-incidence reduction:** Theorem 6.1 (lines 703-770) isolates the factorisation-specific diagonal twist and then invokes exactly the classical signless-incidence facts needed. The factor \(\prod_i d_i^{\deg_H(i)-1}\), the tree degree-balance, and the \(2^{c-1}\) odd-cycle contribution are mutually consistent, including the isolated-tree-vertex cancellation.

2. **Substantive cyclicity theorem:** The nontrivial part of Theorem 7.1 (lines 890-961) is not the name \(h\), but the proof that every primitive \(p\)-primary cokernel has at most one nontrivial invariant factor. The modular rank argument correctly gives rank at least \(k-2\) at every prime, hence unit first \(k-2\) determinantal divisors.

3. **Useful and correct bad-prime support:** Corollary 7.2 (lines 967-989) gives a concise residue criterion that is not tautological. The odd-prime two-support/equal-residue condition and the characteristic-two parity condition follow correctly from the one-dimensional possible modular kernel.

4. **Scheme-aware interpretation:** Lines 901-918 correctly distinguish a finite diagonalizable group scheme from its geometric points in bad characteristic. This is important: \(\mu_{p^a}\) is non-étale and nonreduced in characteristic \(p\), even when its geometric-point set is small.

5. **Responsible contribution boundary:** Sections 10.1-10.4 and the conclusion do not claim novelty for classical signless-incidence minors or the square multiplication Jacobian. This gives the arithmetic result room to be assessed on its actual merits.

### Weaknesses (3-5 items)

1. **The final invariant is defined rather than calculated:** At lines 879-888, \(h(\mathbf e)\) is the top determinantal divisor by definition. Once cyclicity is proved, lines 952-955 identify the final Smith entry with that divisor, which is formal Smith theory. Thus “Smith shape” is an accurate theorem title, but “complete-edge Smith calculation” in the abstract (lines 35-37) and similar headline language are stronger than the current result. Either rename the headline as an **almost-cyclic cokernel and bad-prime-support theorem**, or compute the valuations of \(h\).

2. **Missing arithmetic-matroid and toric-arrangement framework:** The edge characters form a list in the lattice \(X^*(T)\). The quantity \(h=m(E)\) is precisely the full-list multiplicity of the associated representable arithmetic matroid; the equality with a gcd of basis determinants is its standard GCD rule. This framework also identifies the common character kernel with the finite component/multiplicity data of a toric arrangement. Its absence makes a standard invariant look newly introduced and prevents Theorem 6.1 from being recognised as an explicit formula for basis multiplicities.

3. **Full-edge saturation is not the same as a square resultant chart:** Section 7 studies the row lattice generated by all \(\binom{k}{2}\) edge characters, whereas Corollary 8.1 uses a chosen square character matrix. These are different optimisation problems. For \(\mathbf e=(1,2,2)\), the three \(2\times2\) maximal minors have absolute values \(3,2,2\). Hence \(h=1\), so the full edge list is saturated, but no pair of individual resultant characters is unimodular. A Smith row reduction can use integer combinations of all edge characters; on the coprime open set these correspond naturally to Laurent monomials, possibly with negative exponents, not necessarily to globally polynomial resultant normalisers. The manuscript should separate individual resultants, polynomial monomials, Laurent units, and arbitrary semi-invariants.

4. **The stated valuation gap appears readily closable:** Lines 991-993 and 1295-1300 leave exact \(p\)-adic valuations as an exponentially large graph-minor gcd. A local star elimination appears to give a one-line cyclic presentation and a closed valuation formula (detailed below). If correct, leaving this out substantially understates the arithmetic theorem and weakens the paper's main claim unnecessarily.

5. **The critical/sandpile analogy needs a boundary:** The cokernel here is not, as presently defined, a graph critical group: it is not the cokernel of a reduced Laplacian, and no canonical monodromy pairing or divisor interpretation is supplied. Critical-group literature offers useful Smith techniques and comparison questions, but the closer established home is arithmetic matroids/matroids over \(\mathbb Z\) and toric arrangements. The paper should say this explicitly if “critical-group” language is retained in the future-work discussion (line 1336).

### Detailed Comments

#### Location-specific theorem stress tests

1. **Theorem 6.1, lines 666-778 — correct reduction, but place it in signed-graphic language.** Let \(B_H\) be the unoriented incidence matrix. It is the incidence representation of the all-negative signed graph, so its independent sets and odd-unicyclic components are naturally described by the frame matroid. Appending the degree row produces a one-row extension/coextension whose only missing direction on a tree component is the bipartition vector; pairing that vector with \(\mathbf d\) gives the displayed balance. The theorem's exact weighted formula is useful, but its support classification is more naturally “classical frame-matroid incidence plus one degree row” than a standalone new graph classification. The present disclaimer at lines 698-701 is good; adding Zaslavsky's signed-graph formulation would make it conceptually complete.

2. **Theorem 7.1, lines 879-961 — meaningful but partly tautological.** The proof of full rank, modular corank at most one, and primitive cyclicity is sound. The sentence “the final Smith entry is ... \(h\)” is tautological because \(h\) was defined to be the gcd of maximal minors. This does not make the theorem empty: “all earlier primitive invariant factors are one” is a genuine structural statement. I suggest changing the theorem's lead statement to “the primitive complete-edge cokernel is finite cyclic” and presenting the nonprimitive Smith form as its scaled consequence. This communicates the actual content more directly.

3. **Corollary 7.2, lines 963-1000 — elevate it.** The support criterion is currently the most explicit new arithmetic conclusion. It deserves either theorem status or incorporation into a stronger local-Smith theorem. Its proof is correct in all support-size cases. In particular, for odd \(p\), the only surviving case is exactly two \(p\)-units with equal residues; for \(p=2\), the surviving kernel is the parity vector on the odd-degree indices.

4. **A proposed complete local presentation.** Write

   \[
   M=X^*(T)=\mathbb Z^k/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1),\qquad
   C_{\mathbf e}=M/\langle e_jx_i+e_ix_j:i<j\rangle.
   \]

   Fix a prime \(p\) and choose \(a\) with \(p\nmid e_a\), possible because \(\mathbf e\) is primitive. Over \(\mathbb Z_{(p)}\), the star-edge relations give

   \[
   x_i=-\frac{e_i}{e_a}x_a\qquad(i\ne a).
   \]

   The ambient relation \(\sum_i x_i=0\) gives

   \[
   \left(e_a-\sum_{i\ne a}e_i\right)x_a=0,
   \]

   and each remaining non-star edge \(ij\), with \(i,j\ne a\), gives

   \[
   2e_ie_jx_a=0
   \]

   up to the unit \(e_a^{-1}\). Therefore the manuscript should check and, if correct, prove the local isomorphism

   \[
   \boxed{
   C_{\mathbf e}\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}
   \cong
   \mathbb Z_{(p)}\Big/
   \left(e_a-\sum_{i\ne a}e_i,\;2e_ie_j\ (i<j,\ i,j\ne a)\right).
   }
   \]

   This immediately proves local cyclicity and gives the closed formula

   \[
   v_p(h(\mathbf e))=
   \min\left\{
   v_p\!\left(e_a-\sum_{i\ne a}e_i\right),
   \min_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\ne a}}
   \bigl(v_p(2)+v_p(e_i)+v_p(e_j)\bigr)
   \right\},
   \]

   with \(v_p(0)=\infty\). For odd \(p\) in the exceptional two-unit case \(S=\{a,b\}\), this simplifies to

   \[
   v_p(h)=\min\left\{v_p(e_a-e_b),
   \min_{i\notin\{a,b\}}v_p(e_i)\right\}.
   \]

   The same presentation gives the exact \(2\)-adic valuation and recovers the parity support criterion. This would turn Theorem 7.1 from a Smith-shape statement with an implicit top divisor into a complete prime-by-prime Smith calculation. It also gives a sparse presentation, answering one of the paper's own next-step questions.

5. **Lines 1002-1009 — add the \((1,2,2)\) example.** This example is more diagnostic than the three currently listed. It shows simultaneously that the full lattice can be saturated and that no edge basis is unimodular. It should be used to prevent readers from treating \(h\) as the minimum residual degree among raw resultant subsets.

6. **Corollary 8.1, lines 1034-1066 — state which lattice datum is being used.** The degree formula correctly uses the determinant of a selected square matrix \(W\), not \(h(W_E)\). A sentence should say that \(h\) governs the common kernel of the complete redundant edge list, whereas a raw \(k-1\)-resultant normalisation has degree \(|\det W_H|\). If Laurent monomial changes among all resultants are allowed on \(D(\Delta)\), Smith equivalence becomes available; if global polynomial normalisers are required, it generally does not.

7. **Contribution wording, lines 1273-1281 and 1351-1379.** The strongest defensible current phrasing is “almost-cyclic complete-edge cokernel and exact bad-prime support,” not “complete Smith calculation.” If the local presentation above is proved, then “complete Smith calculation” becomes justified and should lead both the abstract and conclusion.

#### Assumption Audit

- **Explicit assumptions:** Theorems 6.1 and 7.1 assume positive integer degrees, \(k\ge3\), a labelled complete graph, and the product-one torus. Corollary 7.2 additionally uses the primitive vector \(\mathbf e\). These are appropriate. The basis \(e_i-e_k\) of the cocharacter hyperplane is integral, so no hidden factor \(k\) enters; lines 723-736 correctly address this common lattice pitfall.

- **Implicit assumptions:** The manuscript implicitly moves between four categories of normalisers: individual pairwise resultants, nonnegative resultant monomials, Laurent monomials that are units on \(D(\Delta)\), and arbitrary semi-invariants. Smith row operations are legitimate for the character lattice and for Laurent units, but can introduce negative exponents and therefore need not preserve globally polynomial normalisers. This distinction materially affects what “fixing the torus” means. The base of the diagonalizable group scheme \(D(X^*(T)/L)\) is also left implicit; it should be stated as a group scheme over \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\) followed by base change, or over a specified field.

- **Paradigmatic assumptions:** The paper treats an integer character list primarily through a single matrix and its top minors. From arithmetic combinatorics, the natural object is the represented arithmetic matroid (or more finely, a matroid over \(\mathbb Z\)/over each DVR), whose multiplicity function records saturation for every subset. This shift would distinguish basis-level chart data \(m(H)=|\det W_H|\), full-list data \(m(E)=h\), and local \(p\)-primary module structure without conflating them.

#### Cross-Disciplinary Connections

- **Parallel research:** Lists of torus characters with nontrivial lattice indices are the basic input of toric-arrangement and arithmetic-matroid theory. In that language, \(h\) is not an ad hoc new invariant but \(m(E)\), the multiplicity of the complete list, and Theorem 6.1 computes multiplicities of bases. Signed-graph theory supplies the frame-matroid explanation of odd-unicyclic incidence bases. Critical-group theory studies related Smith phenomena, but for Laplacian cokernels rather than this edge-character quotient.

- **Borrowing opportunities:** Define the arithmetic matroid of the resultant-character list and record \(m(H)\) for every full-rank edge set. Theorem 6.1 then becomes an explicit basis-multiplicity theorem; Theorem 7.1 becomes a statement that the full-list torsion module is almost cyclic. It would also be natural to define two distinct chart invariants: \(h=m(E)\), the saturation index of all characters, and \(\min_H m(H)\), the smallest residual degree attainable by a subset of individual resultants.

- **Methodological borrowing:** Use local presentations over \(\mathbb Z_{(p)}\) or the matroid-over-a-DVR viewpoint instead of enumerating maximal minors. For arbitrary subgraphs \(G\subseteq K_k\), deletion-contraction and signed-graphic decomposition may yield recursive Smith data. An arithmetic Tutte polynomial could package ranks and all saturation multiplicities, although computing it is a longer-term extension rather than a prerequisite for this paper.

#### Practical Impact

- **Real-world application:** No policy or social application is needed for this pure-mathematics paper. Its concrete mathematical use is to decide whether proposed resultant normalisers span the torus character lattice, what finite diagonalizable kernel remains, and in which characteristics that kernel ceases to be étale.

- **Implementation feasibility:** The present definition of \(h\) requires a gcd over exponentially many \((k-1)\)-edge subsets. The local star presentation above would reduce this to \(O(k^2)\) integer valuations for a chosen \(p\)-unit vertex, making the result computationally useful. For chart construction, the authors should expose separately: full-lattice saturation, best square edge subset, and availability of polynomial versus Laurent character combinations.

- **Stakeholders:** The relevant mathematical audiences are elimination theorists, toric geometers, combinatorial matrix theorists, arithmetic-matroid researchers, and developers of exact computer-algebra or proof-assistant libraries. No affected human population or policy stakeholder arises.

#### Broader Implications

- **Ethical dimensions:** There are no substantive research-ethics concerns beyond accurate attribution and claim calibration. The manuscript is commendably explicit about producer-side verification and bounded priority evidence.

- **Social impact:** No material social-impact claim is appropriate. Forcing one would distract from the mathematical contribution.

- **Future directions:** The highest-value immediate direction is a complete local Smith theorem, followed by: (i) the arithmetic matroid and arithmetic Tutte polynomial of the complete resultant-character list; (ii) arbitrary normaliser graphs \(G\), with connectedness and saturation criteria; (iii) a classification of when an individual edge basis is unimodular versus when only the full list is saturated; (iv) semigroup-versus-lattice normaliser questions; and (v) only then, a careful search for any genuine critical-group or arithmetical-graph model. The Level-3 Smith-normal-form programme remains the strongest credible endpoint, but it should be reframed as an exact arithmetic-matroid/local-cokernel theory rather than as the mere replacement of one degree difference by an unspecified determinantal gcd.

### Questions for Authors

1. Will you define the arithmetic matroid represented by the edge characters and identify \(h=m(E)\) explicitly, so that the standard part of the GCD construction is separated from your specific cyclicity and residue theorem?

2. Can you verify the \(p\)-local star presentation displayed above? If it is correct, is there any reason not to replace the maximal-minor valuation gap with the resulting complete valuation formula?

3. Which normalisers are allowed in each geometric claim: individual resultants, polynomial resultant monomials, Laurent units on \(D(\Delta)\), or arbitrary semi-invariants? Which Smith operations remain geometric in each category?

4. In view of \(\mathbf e=(1,2,2)\), what invariant governs the least residual degree obtainable from exactly \(k-1\) individual resultants? Do you intend to study \(\min_H|\det W_H|\) separately from \(h\)?

5. Is the rational matroid of the augmented signless-incidence representation best described as a frame-matroid extension/coextension depending on the degree row? An explicit identification would make the component criterion in Theorem 6.1 immediately recognisable.

6. Does the gcd of the spanning-tree determinants alone always equal \(h\)? Small examples suggest that this may hold, but the manuscript neither states nor uses it. A proof or counterexample would clarify how much the odd-unicyclic minors contribute arithmetically.

7. Over which base is \(D(X^*(T)/L)\) meant in Theorem 7.1? Can the text state explicitly the connected/étale decomposition after base change to characteristic \(p\)?

### Minor Issues

- At lines 909-914, write \(\mu_{g h(\mathbf e)}\) at first occurrence rather than \(\mu_{gh}\), or explicitly say that \(h=h(\mathbf e)\).

- At lines 887-888, “finite explicit gcd” is mathematically explicit but computationally exponential; “finite determinantal gcd” would be more neutral unless an efficient reduction is supplied.

- The abstract and conclusion should avoid “Smith calculation” until the valuations of \(h\) are supplied. “Smith shape, cyclic primitive cokernel, and exact bad-prime support” is accurate now.

- The term “torus isogeny” should be reserved for a square full-rank character map or for the map onto its scheme-theoretic image. The full edge map has a higher-dimensional target.

- Add “arithmetic matroid,” “toric arrangement,” and possibly “signed graph” to the keywords if the recommended reframing is adopted.

- State once whether matrices list characters by rows and whether cokernels are taken from the row lattice; this will prevent transpose-convention confusion for Smith-form readers.

- The manuscript's critical-group future-work phrase should be qualified: no critical-group identification is presently proved.
