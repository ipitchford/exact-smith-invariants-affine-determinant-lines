## Devil's Advocate Review

The manuscript is unusually candid about its classical antecedents, bounded novelty claim, and the difference between producer-side checks and independent verification. Its integral formulations, explicit orientations, and careful separation of local determinant control from global affine geometry are genuine strengths.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

Strip away the programme-level rhetoric and the manuscript may be only a careful formal synthesis. The multiplication-Jacobian scalar and coprimality criterion are classical; the affine Plücker vector is conceded to be formal once one chart minor and the torus kernel are known; multi-bordering is Laplace/Cauchy--Binet; Theorem 6.1 is a weighted specialisation of classical signless-incidence minors; and Corollary 8.1 multiplies a classical root-partition degree by the standard degree of a torus isogeny. The claimed centre of gravity, Theorem 7.1, then calls its last invariant \(h(\mathbf e)\) after defining \(h\) to be exactly the gcd of maximal minors. Once the first \(k-2\) primitive determinantal divisors are shown to be one, the last Smith entry being \(h\) is the definition of Smith determinantal divisors, not a classification. Corollary 7.2 adds a useful rank-drop criterion and therefore exactly determines prime support, but it does not determine valuations, the numerical order of \(\mu_h\), or a sparse generating basis.

Worse, the complete-edge matrix and Corollary 8.1 concern different normalisation data: the former is rectangular and uses all edge characters, whereas the latter requires a chosen square character matrix. A gcd of maximal minors need not be attained by any maximal minor, so the full-lattice index is not automatically the degree of any polynomial chart built from \(k-1\) selected resultants. Nor is the resultant family proved canonical among all semi-invariants. Consequently the paper establishes correct local determinant bookkeeping, but not an intrinsic classification of normalisers, a global affine slice, a Keller map, or a Hessian consequence. Without an exact evaluation or intrinsic use of \(h\), its durable significance may be too slight for a full research article.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. The parsimonious explanation is that the paper is weighted signless-incidence Smith theory with binary-form resultants supplying the weights. Developing the matrix theorem first, for a broader class of weight vectors or graphs, could reveal whether anything genuinely factorisation-specific survives.
2. Instead of defining the final invariant by a gcd, derive a prime-by-prime valuation formula, a sparse lattice basis, or a critical-group/toric interpretation of \(h(\mathbf e)\). Corollary 7.2 then becomes the radical of a genuinely explicit arithmetic classification.
3. Prove an intrinsic unit-character theorem on the pairwise-coprime locus, or classify semi-invariant normalisers up to Smith equivalence. This would explain whether resultant characters are canonical and when their residual group is unavoidable.
4. Connect the rectangular complete-edge map to geometry directly: describe the map using all resultant values and its image torus, or construct explicit square polynomial normalisers realising a basis of its row lattice. Only then use Corollary 8.1 to obtain a degree attached to Theorem 7.1 rather than to an unrelated selected matrix.

### Unexamined Premise (if detected by Frame-Lock Detection)
**MAJOR.** The paper proceeds as though the full family of pairwise resultants is a privileged normalisation system whose character cokernel measures an intrinsic residual ambiguity. That premise is not established. Resultants are canonical detectors of inter-factor collision, but canonicity of their *characters as normalisers* does not follow; simple coefficient semi-invariants already give different, even unimodular, character lattices on smaller dense charts. Without a universal property tied to regular units on the coprime locus, boundary divisors, symmetry, or descent, the Smith group is best understood as an invariant of a chosen edge family rather than of the factorisation map itself. This frame-lock affects the interpretation and significance, not the correctness of the lattice computation.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- Corollary 7.2 is a real non-tautological gain: it converts the modular kernel analysis into an exact prime-support criterion. It partially, but not fully, answers the objection to the definition of \(h\).
- Corollary 8.1's dominance, generic-degree, separable/inseparable, and pointwise étaleness conclusions appear to follow within its stated fixed-degree, labelled, algebraically closed, semi-invariant scope. They do not imply global finiteness, an affine slice, a Keller map, or a Hessian result, and the manuscript correctly says so.
- The manuscript's explicit antecedent and assurance boundaries materially reduce the risk of a false novelty or verification claim; the remaining objection is whether the bounded increment is substantial enough.
