# Geometry-interface revisions for Stage 4

## Scope and status

This note resolves the geometry-interface items in P1-3 of the Stage 3
editorial roadmap. It was prepared against the frozen manuscript, the
Methodology and Perspective reviews, the Devil's Advocate report, and the
editorial decision. It does **not** edit the manuscript, package, or public
outputs.

The main conclusions are:

1. on the universal pairwise-coprime open, the pairwise resultants generate
   the entire group of regular units modulo constants;
2. the rectangular all-edge character map is finite locally free of degree
   the full-list index onto its scheme-theoretic image torus;
3. a square Laurent-resultant chart realising that index always exists, while
   a square chart made from individual resultants or nonnegative monomials
   need not;
4. the post-base-change multiplicity-one claim has a precise Cartier-divisor
   or DVR formulation; and
5. Corollary 8.1 follows from an fpqc-local finite-locally-free diagram, which
   also proves the total, separable, and inseparable degrees without point
   counting.

Throughout, put \(t=k-1\), let

\[
 A_{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z[a_{i,r}:1\leq i\leq k,\ 0\leq r\leq d_i]
\]

be the universal coefficient ring, write \(R_{ij}\) for the generic pairwise
resultants, let \(E=E(K_k)\), and set

\[
 \Delta=\prod_{ij\in E}R_{ij},\qquad
 X=\operatorname{Spec}A_{\mathbb Z},\qquad U=D(\Delta)\subset X.
\]

The resultants are primitive irreducibles and are pairwise nonassociate in the
UFD \(A_{\mathbb Z}\), as already used in Section 4.1 of the manuscript.

## 1. Exact units on the pairwise-coprime open

### Theorem 1 (unit group and boundary-divisor basis)

There is a canonical valuation isomorphism

\[
 \frac{\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)^\times}{\{\pm1\}}
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \bigoplus_{ij\in E}\mathbb Z[R_{ij}],
 \qquad
 u\longmapsto
 \bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{(R_{ij})}(u)\bigr)_{ij\in E}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)^\times
 =\left\{\varepsilon\prod_{ij\in E}R_{ij}^{n_{ij}}:
 \varepsilon\in\{\pm1\},\ n_{ij}\in\mathbb Z\right\}.
 }
\tag{1.1}
\]

The expression is unique. After replacing \(\mathbb Z\) by a field \(K\),
the same statement holds with \(K^\times\) in place of \(\{\pm1\}\).

#### Proof

Since \(U\) is principal affine,

\[
 \Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)=A_{\mathbb Z}[\Delta^{-1}].
\]

Let \(u=f/\Delta^N\) be a unit, and write an inverse as
\(v=g/\Delta^M\). Then

\[
 fg=\Delta^{N+M}.
\]

Unique factorisation, together with irreducibility and pairwise
nonassociateness of the \(R_{ij}\), implies that neither \(f\) nor \(g\) has
an irreducible factor outside the set \(\{R_{ij}\}\). Thus \(u\) has the
form in (1.1). Uniqueness follows by taking the valuations at the distinct
height-one primes \((R_{ij})\). The field version is identical. \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.1 (four normaliser categories)

The categories conflated in the frozen draft separate as follows.

1. **Individual resultant normalisers** are the generators \(R_{ij}\), one
   for each irreducible boundary component.
2. **Polynomial elements that become units on \(U\)** are exactly

   \[
   \varepsilon\prod R_{ij}^{n_{ij}},\qquad n_{ij}\geq0.
   \]

   Their characters are nonnegative integral combinations of edge
   characters.
3. **All regular units on \(U\)** are the Laurent monomials (1.1), with
   arbitrary integral exponents. Their characters are the full row lattice
   generated by the edge characters.
4. **Arbitrary polynomial semi-invariants** are much more numerous and are
   not classified by Theorem 1. For example, a chosen coefficient of each
   \(A_1,\ldots,A_{k-1}\) gives the standard character basis, but those
   coefficients are not units on \(U\); making them units requires deleting
   additional divisors.

All regular units on \(U\) are automatically \(T\)-semi-invariant, since the
right side of (1.1) is a Laurent product of semi-invariants.

### Canonicity consequence

The pairwise resultants are canonical **as prime equations of the boundary of
the fixed open \(U\)**, up to base units; with the usual primitive resultant
normalisation they are fixed elements. More invariantly, their divisor
classes form the canonical basis of the free boundary-divisor group in
Theorem 1. Therefore the lattice

\[
 L_{\mathrm{unit}}
 =\{\text{\(T\)-characters occurring in }\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)^\times\}
 \subset X^*(T)
\]

is exactly the complete resultant-character lattice \(L\). This gives the
missing universal property:

\[
 \boxed{L=L_{\mathrm{unit}}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

It is intrinsic to the \(T\)-action on the specified open \(U=D(\Delta)\).
It is not intrinsic among arbitrary semi-invariants, nor after shrinking
\(U\) further. Every residual-group statement should therefore say
"residual group for regular-unit normalisation on the pairwise-coprime open"
or, more briefly, "unit-character residual group of \(U\)."

This is stronger than merely calling the group "resultant-list-relative,"
but narrower than claiming a classification of all semi-invariants.

## 2. The rectangular all-edge character map

Let \(M=X^*(T)\cong\mathbb Z^t\). Define

\[
 \alpha:\mathbb Z^E\longrightarrow M,
 \qquad e_{ij}\longmapsto\chi_{ij},
 \qquad L=\operatorname{im}\alpha.
\]

Write \(D_S(N)=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_S[N]\) for the diagonalizable
group over a base scheme \(S\) with character group \(N\). Thus
\(T=D_S(M)\) and \((\mathbb G_m)^E=D_S(\mathbb Z^E)\).

### Theorem 2 (scheme-theoretic image and kernel)

Over \(S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\), and compatibly after arbitrary base
change, the all-edge character morphism

\[
 \chi_E:T=D_S(M)\longrightarrow D_S(\mathbb Z^E)=(\mathbb G_m)^E
\]

has scheme-theoretic image

\[
 I_E=D_S(L)\hookrightarrow(\mathbb G_m)^E.
\]

It factors as

\[
 T\xrightarrow{\ \bar\chi_E\ }I_E
 \hookrightarrow(\mathbb G_m)^E,
\tag{2.1}
\]

where \(\bar\chi_E\) is faithfully flat and finite locally free of degree

\[
 [M:L]=|M/L|.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Its kernel is the finite diagonalizable group scheme

\[
 \ker\bar\chi_E=D_S(M/L).
\tag{2.3}
\]

For a primitive degree vector, \([M:L]=h(\mathbf e)\), so the kernel has
scheme order \(h(\mathbf e)\). For \(\mathbf d=g\mathbf e\), its order is

\[
 g^t h(\mathbf e)=g^{k-1}h(\mathbf e),
\]

in agreement with the product of the Smith entries in Theorem 7.1.

#### Proof

The coordinate Hopf-algebra map is

\[
 \mathbb Z[\mathbb Z^E]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[M],
 \qquad X^v\longmapsto X^{\alpha(v)}.
\]

Its image is the group algebra \(\mathbb Z[L]\), and its kernel is generated
by the binomials \(X^v-X^w\) with \(\alpha(v)=\alpha(w)\). Hence its
scheme-theoretic image is \(D_S(L)\). The inclusion \(L\hookrightarrow M\)
has finite cokernel. Choosing coset representatives for \(M/L\) gives a
direct-sum decomposition

\[
 \mathbb Z[M]=\bigoplus_{\bar m\in M/L}\mathbb Z[L]X^m,
\]

so \(\mathbb Z[M]\) is finite free of rank \([M:L]\) over
\(\mathbb Z[L]\). This proves finite local freeness, and positive constant
rank gives faithful flatness. Taking the fibre above the identity gives
\(D_S(M/L)\). The same group-algebra basis proves compatibility with every
base change. \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.1 (a square Laurent-unit chart always realises the index)

Choose a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_t\) of \(L\). Since every
\(\ell_a\) is an integral combination of the edge characters, choose
\(z_{a,ij}\in\mathbb Z\) with

\[
 \ell_a=\sum_{ij}z_{a,ij}\chi_{ij},
 \qquad
 u_a=\prod_{ij}R_{ij}^{z_{a,ij}}\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)^\times.
\]

Then the square character matrix \(W_L\) of \(u_1,\ldots,u_t\) satisfies

\[
 |\det W_L|=[M:L].
\]

Thus Smith row operations are always geometric in the category of Laurent
units on \(U\). Over the squarefree target open, the normalised
factorisation map

\[
 (m_{\mathbf d},u_1,\ldots,u_t):
 U\longrightarrow V_D\times(\mathbb G_m)^t
\]

has generic degree

\[
 [M:L]\frac{D!}{\prod_i d_i!}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

For primitive degrees this is \(h(\mathbf e)D!/\prod_i d_i!\).

The Laurent functions need not extend polynomially across \(X\setminus U\).
The existence of a basis of \(L\) therefore does **not** imply that some
\(k-1\) individual resultants, or even some nonnegative resultant monomials,
realise this degree as a globally polynomial chart.

### Proposition 2.2 (degree of the rectangular map onto its image)

Let \(B=V_D^{\mathrm{sf}}\), let \(\pi:Z\to B\) be the labelled
root-partition cover, and let \(q:P=m^{-1}(B)\to Z\) be the
\(T\)-torsor used in Section 8. The all-resultant tuple

\[
 r_E=(R_e)_{e\in E}:P\longrightarrow(\mathbb G_m)^E
\]

is \(\chi_E\)-equivariant. The scheme-theoretic image
\(\mathcal I_E\) of

\[
 (q,r_E):P\longrightarrow Z\times(\mathbb G_m)^E
\]

is an \(I_E\)-torsor over \(Z\), fpqc-locally a translate of
\(I_E\subset(\mathbb G_m)^E\). The induced map

\[
 P\longrightarrow\mathcal I_E
\]

is a torsor under \(D(M/L)\), hence is finite locally free of degree
\([M:L]\).

#### Proof

Base-change by the fpqc cover \(q:P\to Z\). The torsor identity identifies
the pullback with the family of maps

\[
 T\longrightarrow(\mathbb G_m)^E,\qquad
 \lambda\longmapsto r_E(x)\chi_E(\lambda)
\]

parametrised by \(x\in P\). By Theorem 2, each scheme-theoretic image is the
translate \(r_E(x)I_E\), and the map onto it is a
\(D(M/L)\)-torsor of finite locally free degree \([M:L]\). These translated
closed subgroup schemes and the torsor maps satisfy the descent cocycle
because \(r_E\) is equivariant. Effective fpqc descent gives
\(\mathcal I_E\) and the claimed finite locally free map. \(\square\)

Thus, on a fixed projective factorisation branch, the tuple of all resultant
values maps the scaling torus onto a translate of \(I_E\), with degree
\([M:L]\); Proposition 2.2 globalises this statement over the branch-labelled
cover.

One must not infer a degree of
\([M:L]D!/\prod_i d_i!\) for the raw rectangular morphism into
\(V_D\times(\mathbb G_m)^E\). Different root-partition branches generally
give different translates of \(I_E\); their possible identifications have not
been analysed. Formula (2.4) is the correct direct total-degree statement,
obtained after choosing a square Laurent-unit basis of \(L\). It forces every
branch to map surjectively to the same \(t\)-dimensional target torus.

## 3. Square edge charts and the diagnostic vector \((1,2,2)\)

For a \(t\)-edge set \(H\subset E\), the individual-resultant chart uses
\((R_e)_{e\in H}\) and has character matrix \(W_H\). If
\(\det W_H\ne0\), Corollary 8.1 gives

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{gen}}(m,(R_e)_{e\in H})
 =|\det W_H|\frac{D!}{\prod_i d_i!}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The integer \(|\det W_H|\) is the arithmetic-matroid basis multiplicity
\(m(H)\). By contrast,

\[
 h=m(E)=\gcd_Hm(H)
\]

is the index of the redundant full list. A gcd need not be attained by a
single basis.

More generally, if \(A=(a_{re})\in M_{t\times E}(\mathbb Z_{\geq0})\), then

\[
 g_r=\prod_eR_e^{a_{re}}
\]

are globally polynomial resultant monomials and their character matrix is
\(AW_E\). Allowing \(A\in M_{t\times E}(\mathbb Z)\) gives all Laurent-unit
tuples on \(U\). Arbitrary integral row reduction belongs to the latter
category, not automatically to the former.

### Proposition 3 (the \((1,2,2)\) separation example)

For the primitive degree vector \(\mathbf e=(1,2,2)\), in the cocharacter
basis \((e_1-e_3,e_2-e_3)\) and edge order \(12,13,23\),

\[
 W_E=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 2&1\\
 1&-1\\
 -2&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The three maximal minors are \(-3,2,-2\). Hence

\[
 h(1,2,2)=\gcd(3,2,2)=1,
\]

but no pair of individual resultant characters is unimodular. The three raw
edge charts have residual orders \(3,2,2\), respectively.

Nevertheless a unimodular **polynomial monomial** chart exists in this
example. Set

\[
 g_1=R_{12}R_{13}R_{23},\qquad
 g_2=R_{12}R_{23}.
\]

Indeed,

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}W_E
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since \(D=5\), the root-partition degree is

\[
 \nu=\frac{5!}{1!2!2!}=30.
\]

Thus the monomial chart \((m,g_1,g_2)\) has generic degree \(30\) and is
étale on \(D(\Delta)\) in every characteristic. The three individual-edge
charts have generic degrees \(90,60,60\), subject in each characteristic to
the determinant criterion.

This example proves three different facts at once:

- full-list saturation does not imply a unimodular individual-edge basis;
- nonnegative monomials can outperform every individual-edge basis; and
- \(h\) is realised geometrically here, but (3.3) is an example-specific
  semigroup fact, not a consequence of saturation alone.

## 4. Multiplicity one after base change

Let \(J=I_t(K)\). The universal ideal identity is

\[
 I_{D+1}(Dm_{\mathbf d})=\Delta J.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because (4.1) is a polynomial ideal identity, for every ring map
\(A_{\mathbb Z}\to B\) one has unconditionally

\[
 I_{D+1}(Dm_{\mathbf d,B})=\Delta_BJ_B.
\tag{4.2}
\]

No reducedness, normality, or transversality assumption is needed for (4.2).

### Proposition 4 (DVR/order formulation)

Assume \(B\) is a Noetherian normal domain and let \(\mathfrak p\subset B\)
be a height-one prime. Write \(v_{\mathfrak p}\) for the normalized valuation
of the DVR \(B_{\mathfrak p}\). Then

\[
 v_{\mathfrak p}\bigl(I_{D+1}(Dm_B)\bigr)
 =\sum_{e\in E}v_{\mathfrak p}(R_{e,B})
  +v_{\mathfrak p}(J_B),
\tag{4.3}
\]

where the valuation of a finitely generated ideal is the minimum valuation of
its generators. In particular, the determinantal ideal has multiplicity one
along \(\mathfrak p\) if

1. \(v_{\mathfrak p}(R_{ij,B})=1\);
2. every \(R_{e,B}\) with \(e\ne ij\) is a unit in
   \(B_{\mathfrak p}\); and
3. some maximal \(K\)-minor is a unit in \(B_{\mathfrak p}\), equivalently
   \((J_B)_{\mathfrak p}=B_{\mathfrak p}\).

Under these hypotheses,

\[
 I_{D+1}(Dm_B)B_{\mathfrak p}
 =(R_{ij,B})B_{\mathfrak p}
 =\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

Localise (4.2) at \(\mathfrak p\). Valuations in a DVR are additive on
products of ideals and take the minimum on a finitely generated ideal, giving
(4.3). The three hypotheses make all terms except the first equal to zero
and make the first equal to one, proving (4.4). \(\square\)

### Proposition 4.1 (Cartier-divisor formulation without normality)

Let \(f:Y\to X\) be a base change, let \(C\subset Y\) be a prime effective
Cartier divisor with generic point \(\eta\), and let \(s\) be a local equation
for \(C\) at \(\eta\). Suppose

\[
 f^*R_{ij}=us\quad\text{in }\mathcal O_{Y,\eta}
\]

for a unit \(u\), every other \(f^*R_e\) is a unit at \(\eta\), and some
maximal \(K\)-minor is a unit there. Then

\[
 I_{D+1}(Dm_Y)\mathcal O_{Y,\eta}
 =(s)=\mathcal I_{C,\eta}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus the rank-loss scheme contains \(C\) with Cartier order exactly one at its
generic point.

This is the correct replacement for "the component is reduced." A ramified
base change can have \(f^*R_{ij}=us^m\), in which case the multiplicity is
\(m\), even if the underlying component is reduced. A second collision or a
vanishing \(K\)-minor contributes the additional terms visible in (4.3).

## 5. Finite-locally-free proof of the generic-degree theorem

Let \(K\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(p\geq0\), let

\[
 B=V_D^{\mathrm{sf}},\qquad
 \pi:Z\longrightarrow B
\]

be the labelled projective root-partition cover, and put

\[
 \nu=\deg\pi=\frac{D!}{\prod_i d_i!}.
\]

The cover \(\pi\) is finite étale of constant degree \(\nu\). Let

\[
 q:P=m^{-1}(B)\longrightarrow Z
\]

be the product-one frame torsor. It is an fpqc \(T\)-torsor. Let
\(g=(g_1,\ldots,g_t)\) be nonzero semi-invariants with square character
matrix \(W\) and \(\det W\ne0\), and put \(G=(\mathbb G_m)^t\).

### Lemma 5 (one common good open)

For each \(a\), the \(T\)-stable zero locus \(V(g_a)\cap P\) descends through
the torsor \(q\) to a closed subscheme \(Z_a\subset Z\). Because \(g_a\) is
nonzero and \(P\) is dense in the irreducible affine coefficient space,
\(Z_a\ne Z\). Since \(Z\) is irreducible and \(\pi\) is finite,
\(\pi(Z_a)\subsetneq B\) is closed. Hence

\[
 B_0=B\setminus\bigcup_a\pi(Z_a)
\tag{5.1}
\]

is dense and open. Put \(Z_0=\pi^{-1}(B_0)\) and
\(P_0=q^{-1}(Z_0)\). Every \(g_a\) is invertible on \(P_0\), so

\[
 g:P_0\longrightarrow G
\]

is defined and satisfies \(g(\lambda x)=\chi_W(\lambda)g(x)\).

For Laurent resultant units, no extra zero locus occurs; one may take
\(B_0=B\).

### Theorem 5.1 (fpqc-local isogeny diagram)

Define

\[
 \theta=(q,g):P_0\longrightarrow Z_0\times G.
\]

Then \(\theta\) is finite locally free of degree
\(n=|\det W|\). Moreover

\[
 \Phi_0=(m,g):P_0\longrightarrow B_0\times G
\]

factors as

\[
 P_0\xrightarrow{\ \theta\ }Z_0\times G
 \xrightarrow{\ \pi\times\mathrm{id}_G\ }B_0\times G,
\tag{5.2}
\]

and is finite locally free of degree \(\nu n\).

#### Proof

Base-change \(\theta\) by the fpqc cover

\[
 q\times\mathrm{id}_G:P_0\times G\longrightarrow Z_0\times G.
\]

The torsor identity \(P_0\times_{Z_0}P_0\cong P_0\times T\) identifies the
resulting Cartesian square with

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 P_0\times T
 &\xrightarrow{\ \beta\ }&P_0\times G\\[2mm]
 \downarrow {a}&&\downarrow {q\times\mathrm{id}_G}\\[2mm]
 P_0&\xrightarrow{\ \theta\ }&Z_0\times G,
\end{array}
\tag{5.3}
\]

where

\[
 a(x,\lambda)=\lambda x,
 \qquad
 \beta(x,\lambda)=\bigl(x,g(x)\chi_W(\lambda)\bigr).
\]

The square is Cartesian: if \(q(y)=q(x)\), the torsor gives a unique
\(\lambda\) with \(y=\lambda x\), and equivariance gives
\(g(y)=\chi_W(\lambda)g(x)\).

Smith normal form and torus automorphisms reduce \(\chi_W\) to

\[
 (z_1,\ldots,z_t)\longmapsto(z_1^{s_1},\ldots,z_t^{s_t}),
\tag{5.4}
\]

where \(\prod_i s_i=|\det W|\). On coordinate rings, each power map is free
of rank \(s_i\), with basis \(1,z_i,\ldots,z_i^{s_i-1}\). Thus
\(\chi_W\), and hence its translate \(\beta\), is finite locally free of
degree \(n\). Finite local freeness and its degree are fpqc local on the
target, so (5.3) proves the assertion for \(\theta\). This is precisely
[Stacks Project, Tag 02VO](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02VO).

The map \(\pi\times\mathrm{id}_G\) is finite étale, hence finite locally
free, of degree \(\nu\). Composition of finite locally free morphisms is
finite locally free ([Stacks Project, Section 29.49, especially Lemmas
29.49.3--4](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02K9)); ranks multiply, which
gives degree \(\nu n\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2 (total, separable, and inseparable degrees)

Write the Smith entries as

\[
 s_i=p^{a_i}s_i',\qquad p\nmid s_i',
\]

with \(a_i=0\) in characteristic zero. Then the function-field extension
induced by \(\Phi\) has

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{tot}}\Phi
 =\nu\prod_i s_i
 =\nu|\det W|,
\tag{5.5}
\]

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{sep}}\Phi
 =\nu\prod_i s_i',
\tag{5.6}
\]

and

\[
 \deg_{\mathrm{insep}}\Phi
 =p^{\sum_i a_i}=p^{v_p(\det W)}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Indeed, the root-partition cover contributes the separable factor \(\nu\).
For (5.4), the extension \(K(z_i)/K(z_i^{s_i})\) has total degree \(s_i\),
separable degree \(s_i'\), and inseparable degree \(p^{a_i}\). Tensoring the
coordinate directions and composing with the étale cover gives
(5.5)--(5.7).

Scheme-theoretically,

\[
 \ker\chi_W\cong\prod_i\mu_{s_i}
\]

has length \(\prod_i s_i\), while its geometric-point count is
\(\prod_i s_i'\). Its connected nonreduced part has length
\(p^{\sum_i a_i}\). The morphism is generically étale exactly when
\(p\nmid\det W\). On the full open
\(D(\Delta\prod_ag_a)\), the bordered determinant proves pointwise étaleness
under the same condition. Étaleness may also be checked fpqc-locally
([Stacks Project, Tag 02VN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02VN)).

This proof establishes lengths and field degrees; it does not infer them from
the number of geometric points of a possibly nonreduced fibre.

## 7. Blockers, source cautions, and Devil's Advocate disposition

### Resolved without a new hypothesis

- The exact unit group of the universal pairwise-coprime open.
- The scheme-theoretic image and finite kernel of the rectangular character
  map.
- Existence of a square Laurent-unit chart with determinant \(m(E)\).
- The \((1,2,2)\) saturated-but-no-edge-basis example, including an explicit
  unimodular nonnegative-monomial chart.
- Precise multiplicity-one hypotheses.
- Scheme-level total, separable, and inseparable degree formulas.

### Remaining boundaries, not theorem blockers

1. The exact unit formula is asserted for the universal UFD and polynomial
   rings over fields. Under an arbitrary base change, resultants can split or
   constants can acquire new units, so the formula must not be copied without
   hypotheses.
2. Saturation of \(L\) does not by itself guarantee a basis made from
   nonnegative resultant monomials. The general semigroup-basis problem
   remains open in this note.
3. The naïve rectangular map into \(V_D\times(\mathbb G_m)^E\) need not have
   degree \(h\nu\) onto its unlabelled image; generic separation of the
   branch translates has not been proved. The branch-labelled degree \(h\)
   and square Laurent-chart degree \(h\nu\) are rigorous substitutes.
4. None of these arguments classifies arbitrary polynomial semi-invariants or
   normalisers after deleting additional coefficient divisors.
5. The irreducibility and pairwise nonassociateness of the universal
   resultants remain inputs from the manuscript's resultant antecedents; the
   unit theorem does not independently reprove that classical fact.

The proofs above are algebraic and self-contained once generic-resultant
irreducibility and the root-partition cover are granted. The only external
descent facts used are:

- finite local freeness and constant degree are fpqc-local on the target:

  [Stacks Project, Tag 02VN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02VN); and
- finite locally free morphisms are stable under base change and composition:
  [Stacks Project, Section 29.49](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02K9).

The manuscript's current generic Stacks citation can retain these exact tags.
The group-scheme image statement does not require an additional citation:
the displayed group-algebra calculation proves it over \(\mathbb Z\) and
after every base change.

### Is the DA canonicity objection fully answered?

**Yes at the exact regular-unit level, and no beyond it.** Theorem 1 proves
the universal property the DA requested: the complete resultant-character
lattice is precisely the character lattice of every regular unit on the fixed
pairwise-coprime open \(U\). Thus the residual group is intrinsic to
\((U,T)\) in the regular-unit category, and Corollary 2.1 gives it a direct
degree interpretation.

The broader assertion that resultants are canonical among **all** polynomial
semi-invariants remains false: coefficient semi-invariants can give a
unimodular character basis after a further shrinking. The revised manuscript
fully answers the objection only if it adopts the scoped phrase
"unit-character residual group of \(U\)" and abandons unqualified claims about
all normalisers.
