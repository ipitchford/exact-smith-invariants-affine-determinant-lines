# Adversarial audit of the local Smith theorem

**Object audited:** `LOCAL_SMITH_THEOREM.md`  
**Audit mode:** read-only mathematical referee pass plus exact falsification
tests  
**Verdict:** **PASS WITH MINOR CLARIFICATIONS**  
**Severity summary:** no critical error; no major error; three minor
presentation/terminology corrections; no counterexample found

## 1. Scope and assurance boundary

This audit reconstructs the lattice presentation, local elimination, Fitting
ideal, Smith-normal-form, valuation, tree-gcd, and group-scheme arguments
independently from the proof note. It also runs fresh exact computations aimed
at falsifying the claims. The computation was independently implemented for
this audit, but it uses the same SymPy Smith-normal-form backend available in
the project environment. It is therefore an **internal adversarial check**, not
independent specialist reproduction, a second-software implementation,
formal verification, novelty resolution, or peer review.

The conclusion is conditional on the note's stated hypotheses:

- $k\geq 3$;
- all degrees are positive integers;
- the local theorem is applied to the primitive degree vector;
- the tree-gcd theorem imports the already-proved tree determinant formula.

Under those hypotheses, I found no invalid module, Fitting, Smith, valuation,
tree, family, or diagonalizable-group inference.

## 2. Strongest attempted counter-argument

The most plausible failure mode is that the one-generator local presentation
computes only the order of the cokernel, or only a quotient of it, after
silently discarding relations. If that happened, the passage from a minimum of
valuations to a cyclic local module, the global Smith form, and the group-scheme
decomposition would all fail together.

That counter-argument does not survive the presentation calculation. Over
$R_p=\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, a $p$-unit pivot $e_a$ makes every star relation

\[
 e_i x_a+e_a x_i=0
\]

a legitimate Tietze elimination of one generator and one relation. Eliminating
all $x_i$ with $i\ne a$ leaves exactly one generator $x_a$. The ambient
relation becomes $D_a x_a=0$, and every remaining non-star edge becomes
$2e_i e_jx_a=0$, up to multiplication by a unit. No relation is discarded
and no nonunit is divided out. Hence the proof identifies the module itself:

\[
 C_{\mathbf e}\otimes R_p
 \cong R_p/(D_a,2e_i e_j:i<j,\ i,j\ne a),
\]

not merely its order. This is the decisive point on which the downstream
claims rest.

## 3. Line-by-line mathematical audit

### 3.1 Character lattice and presentation — correct

The identification

\[
 X^*(\ker(\mathbb G_m^k\to\mathbb G_m))
 =\mathbb Z^k/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)
\]

is correct. With $q_{ij}=e_jx_i+e_ix_j$, quotienting by the row lattice of
$W_E$ is equivalently the cokernel of $W_E^{\mathsf T}$. Thus the zeroth
Fitting ideal is generated by the $(k-1)\times(k-1)$ minors of the rectangular
matrix. The transpose convention in lines 47--56 is both necessary and
correct.

### 3.2 Local star presentation — correct

Lines 76--144 perform valid presentation transformations. Primitivity provides
a $p$-unit $e_a$ for every prime. The substitutions

\[
 x_i=-\frac{e_i}{e_a}x_a
\]

use only units. The ambient relation has coefficient $D_a/e_a$, and a
non-star edge has coefficient $-2e_i e_j/e_a$. Multiplying these coefficients
by units gives precisely the stated ideal $I_{p,a}$. The argument remains
valid at $p=2$, for $k=3$, and when $D_a=0$.

The choice-independence claim is also correct. If $a$ and $b$ are both
$p$-unit pivots, then $x_b=-(e_b/e_a)x_a$; the two cyclic generators differ
by a unit. Each presentation ideal is therefore the annihilator of the same
localized module.

### 3.3 Fitting ideal and exact valuation — correct

For a cyclic presentation $R_p/I_{p,a}$,
$\operatorname{Fitt}_0=I_{p,a}$. Fitting ideals commute with localization,
and the global maximal-minor ideal in $\mathbb Z$ is generated by the gcd of
the maximal minors. Since $R_p$ is a DVR, $I_{p,a}$ is generated by an
element with the least displayed valuation. Consequently

\[
 v_p(h)=
 \min\left(v_p(D_a),
 \min_{i<j,\ i,j\ne a}
       \{v_p(2)+v_p(e_i)+v_p(e_j)\}\right).
\]

This is an equality of Fitting ideals after the module isomorphism has been
proved; it does not infer a module structure from a matching order.

### 3.4 Primewise cyclicity to global Smith form — correct

Every localized $p$-primary component is cyclic of order $p^{m_p}$. The
local presentation is torsion, so the finitely generated group $C_{\mathbf e}$
has rank zero. Its primary components have pairwise coprime orders, and their
direct sum is cyclic. Hence

\[
 C_{\mathbf e}\cong\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{SNF}(W_E)=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,h).
\]

This includes $h=1$, where the displayed cyclic group is the zero group.

### 3.5 Odd-prime simplification — correct

For odd $p$, the local exponent can be positive only when every non-star
product $e_i e_j$ is divisible by $p$. With a unit pivot $a$, this means
there is at most one other unit degree. If there is no other unit, $D_a$ is a
unit. Thus the only exceptional case has exactly two unit indices
$S_p=\{a,b\}$, and positivity additionally requires
$e_a\equiv e_b\pmod p$.

Writing

\[
 q=\min_{c\notin\{a,b\}}v_p(e_c),
 \qquad
 D_a=(e_a-e_b)-\sum_{c\notin\{a,b\}}e_c,
\]

the second term has valuation at least $q$. The elementary DVR identity

\[
 \min(v_p(x+y),q)=\min(v_p(x),q)
 \quad\text{when }v_p(y)\geq q
\]

gives exactly the claimed formula. It remains valid when one of the compared
integers is zero under the convention $v_p(0)=\infty$.

### 3.6 The \(2\)-adic case split — correct

For an odd pivot $a$, $D_a\equiv s\pmod2$, where $s$ is the number of
odd degrees.

- If $s$ is odd, $D_a$ is a unit and $m_2=0$.
- If $s\geq4$ is even, $D_a$ is even and two odd vertices remain outside
  the pivot, producing a non-star coefficient with valuation exactly one.
  Hence $m_2=1$.
- If $s=2$, the least non-star valuation is
  $1+\min_{c\notin\{a,b\}}v_2(e_c)$, giving the stated truncated minimum.

For the two possible odd pivots,
$D_a+D_b=-2\sum_{c\notin\{a,b\}}e_c$. This proves that their valuations,
truncated at the non-star bound, agree. No division by $2$ is hidden in the
argument.

### 3.7 Symmetric global product formula — correct

For an odd prime $p$, $p\mid Q_{ab}$ if and only if the only $p$-unit
degrees are $e_a,e_b$ and they are congruent modulo $p$. Primitivity is
essential here: if $p\mid e_a$, the definition of $Q_{ab}$ would force
$p$ to divide every degree. Thus a fixed odd prime can occur in at most one
$Q_{ab}$, proving that the odd parts are pairwise coprime. Moreover

\[
 v_p(Q_{ab})=
 \min\left(v_p(e_a-e_b),
           \min_{c\notin\{a,b\}}v_p(e_c)\right)=v_p(h).
\]

The separately defined $2$-adic exponent supplies the missing prime. The
product formula therefore follows prime by prime. The $O(k^2)$
arithmetic-operation claim is reasonable: there are $O(k^2)$ excluded pairs,
and each pair-exclusion gcd can be served by a range-gcd data structure or an
interval-gcd table. The note appropriately does not claim unit-cost bit
complexity.

### 3.8 Tree-gcd equality — correct, with one explicit dependency

Assuming the manuscript's tree determinant formula, the star at $a$ has

\[
 \det W_{T_a}=\pm e_a^{k-2}D_a.
\]

Because $e_a$ is a local unit, this places $D_a$ in the tree-minor ideal.
For $i,j\ne a$, the bent star has determinant

\[
 \det W_{T_{a;i,j}}
 =\pm e_a^{k-3}e_i(D_a+2e_j).
\]

Multiplying by a unit and subtracting $e_iD_a$ places $2e_ie_j$ in the
same ideal. Thus one star and one bent star for every unordered non-pivot pair
generate $I_{p,a}$. Conversely, each selected tree determinant is a maximal
minor, so its ideal is contained in the full Fitting ideal $I_{p,a}$. The two
ideals coincide at every prime, which proves equality of their positive global
generators.

The proof correctly shows that odd-unicyclic bases are redundant for the
single full-list gcd, not that they are irrelevant as individual arithmetic-
matroid multiplicities or square-chart determinants.

### 3.9 Nonprimitive scaling — correct

For $\mathbf d=g\mathbf e$, every row scales by $g$. Applying the same
unimodular row and column operations that put $W_E(\mathbf e)$ into Smith
form gives

\[
 \operatorname{SNF}(W_E(\mathbf d))
 =\operatorname{diag}(g,\ldots,g,gh(\mathbf e)).
\]

The divisibility chain already holds. The resulting cokernel and its localized
form have exactly the stated invariant factors. The warning that the local
pivot must be chosen only after removing the content $g$ is necessary.

### 3.10 Diagonalizable group schemes — correct

For a finite abelian character group $C$, $D(C)$ is finite locally free
over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$, and $D(\mathbb Z/n)=\mu_n$. The Smith
form therefore gives, noncanonically,

\[
 D(C_{\mathbf d})\cong
 \mu_g^{k-2}\times\mu_{gh},
\]

of rank $g^{k-1}h$. In characteristic $p$, primary decomposition separates
each factor into a connected $p$-power part and a prime-to-$p$ finite étale
part. The connected length, étale rank, geometric-point count, total rank, and
étaleness criterion in lines 594--629 all follow correctly.

The qualification in lines 631--634 is important: this is the kernel attached
to the complete resultant-character list. It need not be the kernel of an
individual square list of polynomial normalisers, nor an intrinsic invariant
of all possible semi-invariants.

### 3.11 Diagnostic examples and infinite families — correct

Every displayed Smith form and family was checked symbolically and also follows
from the proved formula. In particular,

\[
 h(1,1,N)=N
\]

because the odd part is contributed by $Q_{12}$, while the exactly-two-odd
case contributes $v_2(N)$ when $N$ is even. Likewise

\[
 h(1,1+2^q,2^q)=2^{q+1}
\]

because $D_1=-2^{q+1}$ and the least non-star coefficient has the same
valuation. The nonprimitive example $(2,2,18)$ has Smith form
$\operatorname{diag}(2,18)$.

## 4. Corrections and clarifications before incorporation

These are minor and do not alter any theorem.

1. **Define $h(\mathbf e)$ in Section 1 before first use.** The proof note
   first displays $h$ in Theorem 3.1 and only then describes it as the positive
   generator of the maximal-minor ideal. Add, after defining $W_E$,
   \[
   h(\mathbf e)=\gcd\{ |\det W_H(\mathbf e)|:
   H\subset E(K_k),\ |H|=k-1\}.
   \]
   State that zeros do not affect the gcd and that the local presentation
   proves this integer is positive.
2. **Expose the exact dependency of Theorem 6.1.** State in its theorem line or
   proof preamble that tree-gcd equality uses the previously established tree
   determinant formula. This prevents the strengthened Smith theorem from
   appearing self-contained if extracted from the manuscript.
3. **Use unambiguous characteristic-$p$ terminology.** Replace “maximal
   étale factor” by “prime-to-$p$ étale direct factor” (and, if desired,
   identify the other factor with the identity component). This avoids the
   otherwise harmless ambiguity between a maximal étale subgroup and maximal
   étale quotient.

An optional tightening is to replace the finiteness sentence in Corollary 3.2
by: “The local presentation over any $R_p$ is torsion; after tensoring with
$\mathbb Q$, $C_{\mathbf e}\otimes\mathbb Q=0$. Hence the finitely generated
abelian group $C_{\mathbf e}$ is finite.”

## 5. Fresh exact falsification tests

### 5.1 Method

A fresh inline checker reconstructed the character matrix directly in the
basis obtained by eliminating $x_k=-\sum_{i<k}x_i$. It did not import the
project verification functions. All comparisons used explicit conditional
failures rather than proof-bearing `assert` statements.

The checker compared:

- exact SymPy Smith normal forms against the closed global formula;
- the local valuation formula at every tested prime and every admissible pivot;
- the gcd of every maximal minor against the gcd of spanning-tree minors;
- primitive and nonprimitive invariant factors;
- the two infinite diagnostic families over substantial finite ranges.

## 6. Minimal regression fixtures

The following six fixtures exercise distinct failure modes with little
redundancy.

| Degree vector | Expected Smith form | Essential failure caught |
|---|---|---|
| $(1,2,2)$ | $(1,1)$ | The raw square minors are $3,2,2$: the full list is saturated although no individual resultant pair is unimodular. Catches replacement of a lattice gcd by one selected square chart. |
| $(1,1,9)$ | $(1,9)$ | Maximal minors $9,9,63$. Catches a support-only theorem that loses the higher valuation $v_3(h)=2$, and checks both $3$-unit pivots. |
| $(3,1,2)$ | $(1,4)$ | With pivot $e_a=3$ at $p=2$, $D_a=0$, while the non-star relation gives $m_2=2$. Catches accidental dependence on a nonzero ambient coefficient. |
| $(1,1,1,1)$ | $(1,1,2)$ | Four odd entries force exactly one factor of $2$. Catches omission of the signless-incidence factor $2$. |
| $(1,2,3,4)$ | $(1,1,4)$ | Tree gcd and full-minor gcd are both $4$, while odd-unicyclic maximal minors are present. Catches an incorrect tree/full-ideal comparison. |
| $(2,2,18)$ | $(2,18)$ | Catches applying the primitive pivot theorem directly at a prime dividing the content, or omitting the $g$-scaling in the first invariant factor. |

Recommended mutation controls are: delete the factor $2$ from the non-star
relations; retain only prime support rather than exact valuation; discard
non-star relations when $D_a=0$; replace the full gcd by one square minor; and
apply the primitive formula without first dividing by the content. Each
mutation is killed by at least one fixture above.

## 7. Incorporation verdict

The local Smith theorem is mathematically ready for incorporation, subject to
the three minor clarifications in Section 4 and a later specialist or formal
check appropriate to the project's assurance standard. The strongest safe
claim is:

> For every primitive positive degree vector with $k\geq3$, the complete
> resultant-character cokernel is cyclic, its exact $p$-adic order is the
> minimum of the pivot imbalance and non-star product valuations, and its
> global index is given by the symmetric pair-exclusion formula. Spanning
> trees already generate the full maximal-minor ideal. Scaling by the content
> gives the complete nonprimitive Smith form and the corresponding residual
> diagonalizable group scheme.

This conclusion does not by itself prove novelty, priority, a polynomial
normaliser classification, an affine-slice theorem, or a Keller/Hessian
consequence.
